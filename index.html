HIS 5093 — Cultural Heritage Tourism

This is a proposed course, developed for ENG 6813: Interdisciplinary Teaching by Jordan Kearschner. It has not yet been taught.

This website was built using Claude Code.

Course Overview

This course examines cultural heritage tourism and the history of American leisure, tourism, and mobility as a lens for practicing public history in the digital age. This course is premised on the belief that heritage and history does not need to be monumental to be meaningful – a local pool, family-owned restaurant, or a neighborhood bloc party can be just as important to the community as any listed monument on the National Register of Historic Places. Students will engage with scholarship on memory, visual culture, digital preservation, and cultural heritage management while developing critical literacy around the growing role of generative AI in cultural heritage practice.

Term: Spring 2027 Meets: Tuesdays, 6:00–8:50 PM Level: Graduate
